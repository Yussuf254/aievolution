Senior Chief Mukudi: The Oath, the Prison, and the Liberation of Bunyala

The son of Namwonja swore the Mau Mau oath even in retirement, orchestrating the movement's activities across Bunyala until his arrest and imprisonment at Kajiado Prison in 1954. Released a year later, he stands at the head of a line of brave, intelligent, and resolute sons and grandsons who fought to liberate Bunyala and to build a nation.

Chapter 01The Oath in Retirement

Senior Chief Mukudi, son of Namwonja, swore the Mau Mau oath even in retirement, orchestrating the movement's activities across Bunyala until his arrest and imprisonment at Kajiado Prison in 1954.

The son who had once succeeded his father as headman of Bunyala South carried the family's spirit of resistance into a new struggle. Even in retirement, he took the Mau Mau oath and became a quiet orchestrator of the movement's activities across Bunyala, keeping the cause alive across the length and breadth of the land.

Kajiado Prison, 1954

Arrested for his role in the movement, Senior Chief Mukudi was imprisoned at Kajiado Prison in 1954 and released a year later. The imprisonment could not break the family's resolve.

He was released a year later, his defiance intact. His story became part of the wider anti-colonial legacy of the family — a thread running from the sovereignty of Namwonja to the struggle for freedom.

Chapter 02Peter Okelo and the Sisenye Forest

Mukudi's own son, Peter Okelo, administered the Mau Mau oaths deep in Sisenye Forest.

Where the father organised, the son administered. Peter Okelo conducted the Mau Mau oath-taking ceremonies deep in Sisenye Forest, keeping the movement alive in the shadows of the trees and drawing new members into the struggle for liberation.

From the forests of Sisenye to the banks of exile, the oath passed from father to son and the struggle continued.
— The Namwonja Standard

Once colonial authorities marked him for capture, Peter Okelo was forced into exile in Uganda — another branch of the family driven beyond the border by the fight for freedom, but never silenced.

Exile and Continuity

Forced into exile in Uganda once colonial authorities marked him for capture, Peter Okelo carried the family's spirit of resistance beyond the border, ensuring the struggle endured.

Chapter 03Namwonja the Visionary

Namwonja was a wise, intelligent, and capable visionary — a charismatic peacemaker and inventive king whose ideas consistently served the common good.

His leadership was not merely inherited; it was demonstrated daily through practice and example. He led not by title alone but by the consistency of his actions, the wisdom of his decisions, and the inventiveness of his ideas.

A king who led by practice and example — whose ideas consistently served the common good.
— The Namwonja Standard

It was this visionary foundation that shaped the line that followed him: a father of brave, intelligent, and resolute sons and grandsons who carried his standard into the struggle for liberation.

Chapter 04The Struggle to Liberate Bunyala

He fathered a line of brave, intelligent, and resolute sons and grandsons who stood among the Abanyala in the struggle to liberate Bunyala from Wanga, Luo, and Abasamia domination.

The struggle was waged against Wanga, Luo, and Abasamia domination — a long contest for the freedom and self-determination of the Abanyala people. It was a cause fought not for power alone, but for the dignity and destiny of the community.

They fought for the creation of Bunyala Location — today Bunyala Sub-County — and for the establishment of Busia District on behalf of the Abaluyia and the Teso people.

A Cause Beyond the Family

The goal was the creation of Bunyala Location, today Bunyala Sub-County, and the establishment of Busia District on behalf of the Abaluyia and the Teso people.

Chapter 05The Architects of a Nation's Symbols

That same spirit of nation-building lived on in his grandsons Samwel Sumba Mukudi and Bernaba Mukudi.

As the first Chairman and Clerk of Busia County Council at independence, the two grandsons were the architects behind Kenya's Court of Arms and the cockerel (Jogoo) emblem that came to symbolise the Kenya African National Union (KANU).

The family that had fought to free its own land helped to give a new nation its defining symbols — from the liberation of Bunyala to the emblem of a Kenya free.

The Jogoo

The cockerel (Jogoo) emblem that came to symbolise the Kenya African National Union (KANU) — a lasting emblem of the nation-building spirit the family carried from liberation to independence.
